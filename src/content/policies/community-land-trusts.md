## The Problem

Denver's current "affordable housing" is temporary. Tax credit projects expire after 15-30 years and revert to market rate. Down payment assistance doesn't prevent future displacement when neighborhoods gentrify and taxes rise. The affordable housing we build today disappears tomorrow.

We need permanently affordable housing controlled by the community, not by developers or the market.

### What Denver Currently Does

Denver already has a community land trust: **Elevation Community Land Trust** (formerly Colorado Community Land Trust), which has operated since 2003. Elevation CLT currently holds approximately 300 permanently affordable homes in the Denver metro area. Denver's Department of Housing Stability (HOST) has provided some funding for Elevation CLT acquisitions.

The community land trust model is also gaining traction through new development. In January 2026, the **Urban Land Conservancy (ULC)** opened **The Irving at Mile High Vista**, a 102-unit permanently affordable, all-electric housing community at 3270 W. Colfax Ave. in the West Colfax neighborhood. The Irving is ULC's first ground-up affordable housing development, serving households earning 20-80% of Area Median Income with affordability guaranteed for at least 99 years through ULC's community land trust. The project was one of the first Proposition 123-funded housing developments to break ground and open, received a $4,080,000 loan from Denver HOST, and a $100,000 grant from Denver's Office of Climate Action, Sustainability and Resiliency for its all-electric design featuring rooftop solar and heat pump systems (Colorado Politics, January 22, 2026).

However, the scale is far below what is needed. Elevation CLT adds approximately 20-40 homes per year, and even with projects like The Irving, the city needs to build 30-40 such communities per year to keep up with demand (Mayor Johnston, January 2026). Denver needs approximately 44,000 new affordable units over the next decade (Denver HOST). At the current rate, it would take over 50 years to reach our 2,500-unit target. Our proposal dramatically scales what already works by providing dedicated city funding, connecting CLTs to the vacancy tax revenue stream, and integrating CLTs with the corporate landlord divestiture pipeline.

## Our Solution

### Denver Community Land Trust Initiative

**City-seeded CLTs:** Denver funds 5 Community Land Trusts - one for each geographic region of the city - with $10M each in startup capital ($50M total).

**How CLTs work:**

1. The CLT owns the land. Permanently.
2. Homeowners buy (or rent) the building on the land.
3. When they sell, resale prices are limited by formula - typically allowing the owner to recoup their investment + equity from improvements + a modest appreciation share (e.g., 25% of market appreciation).
4. The next buyer gets the home at an affordable price. Forever.

**Governance:** Each CLT is governed by a tripartite board:

- 1/3 CLT residents
- 1/3 community members (non-resident neighbors)
- 1/3 public representatives (city appointees with housing expertise)

**Acquisition strategy:**

- Right of first purchase when properties in anti-displacement zones are listed for sale
- Acquire properties from corporate landlord divestiture (see: Corporate Landlord Ban)
- Partner with Social Housing Authority for multi-family buildings
- Accept donated properties with tax benefits for donors

**Target:** 2,500 permanently affordable homes within CLTs over 10 years.

**The CLT model has been proven over 50+ years, across multiple countries and continents, at every scale.** It works because it separates the value of the home (which the owner keeps) from the value of the land (which the community holds). This removes speculation while preserving genuine homeownership.

## How We Pay For It

- **Startup capital:** $50M from city bonds, federal HOME program funds, and vacancy tax revenue
- **Ongoing:** CLTs are self-sustaining once established - ground lease fees cover operations
- **Acquisition:** Funded by vacancy tax revenue, corporate landlord divestiture proceeds, and federal grants

## Frequently Asked Questions

**"Do CLTs actually work?"**
Champlain Housing Trust in Burlington, VT has operated since 1984 with over 2,800 homes. Foreclosure rates are 10x lower than conventional mortgages. Homes stay affordable across generations. It works.

**"Is this really homeownership if you can't keep all the profit?"**
You own your home. You build equity. You just can't speculate on the land underneath it. The trade-off: you paid an affordable price going in. The next family deserves the same opportunity. This is homeownership for people who want homes, not for people who want investment vehicles.
